# Daily refill calling — script

The goal of the call is **not** to sell. It is to be the shop that remembers
what the customer takes. Refill calls convert at 3–5x the rate of cold sales
calls, because you are solving a problem they already have.

## Who to call (build this list today)

A notebook is enough. One line per customer:

| Name | Phone | Medicine | Days per strip | Last purchase | Next call date |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ravi | 98xxx | Metformin 500 | 30 | 12 Nov | 9 Dec |

Rules:
- Only message people who have bought from you and agreed to be contacted.
- Never share the list. Never call about a medicine to someone else's number.
- One missed refill is a lost customer. Two is a lost family.

## The call — 40 seconds, no script-reading

> "Good morning, Ravi sir? This is Leo Hospital pharmacy. You bought Metformin
> from us on the 12th. Your one month will finish in three days — should I keep
> the same strip ready for you?"

Then **stop talking**. Let them answer.

Likely replies and what to do:

| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| "Yes, keep it" | "Done sir, it'll be packed. Cash or UPI? Delivery or pickup?" |
| "I still have some" | "No problem — shall I call you on the 20th instead?" *(log the date)* |
| "I get it free from the govt hospital" | "Understood. If you ever need it urgently on a Sunday, we're open — same number." |
| "How much?" | Give the exact price. Never vague. |
| "Don't call me" | "Sorry for the disturbance, sir. I've removed your number." *(actually remove it)* |

Never say "offer", "discount", "scheme" unless it's real. One false claim and
they stop answering forever.

## Timing

- **Best window:** 9:30–11:30 am and 5:00–7:30 pm.
- **Never:** before 8 am, after 8:30 pm, or on a festival day.
- **Volume:** 15–20 calls a day, done properly, beats 100 rushed ones.
- Batch by medicine: all the BP patients on one day, all the thyroid patients
  on another. Same sentence, less thinking.

## After the call

- Log the outcome in the notebook. If you don't log it, you'll call them twice
  and annoy them.
- New customer? Ask permission to save the number and the medicine, right at
  the counter: *"Shall I note your number so I can keep your monthly tablets
  ready?"* If they say no, write "NO" and respect it.
- Every 10th refill customer, ask for a Google review. That is the cheapest
  marketing you will ever do.

## The counter script (this is where the list is built)

> "Sir, this is a monthly medicine. If you give me your number, I'll keep the
> next strip packed and you won't have to wait. We'll only call about this."

That one sentence, said at every monthly-medicine bill, is what makes the
filling-in above possible.
