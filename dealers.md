# Wholesale dealers — who to buy from, and the paperwork that protects you

A pharmacy can only buy from a **licensed wholesale dealer**. Buying from an
unlicensed source is how spurious-drug cases begin, and the retailer is the one
who gets raided, not the supplier.

## Before the first order — verify the dealer

Ask for these five things and keep a photocopy. Refuse if any is missing.

| # | Document | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Drug licence 20B / 21B** | Wholesale licence, valid on today's date, premises address matches the godown |
| 2 | **GSTIN** | Format `32ABCDE1234F1Z5` (Kerala starts with 32) — verify on the GST portal |
| 3 | **Licence holder's ID** | Name on the licence = name on the invoice |
| 4 | **Cold-chain capability** | For insulin, vaccines, injections — ask how they transport |
| 5 | **Return policy in writing** | Near-expiry and damaged stock |

Red flags: "we'll send the invoice later", licence shows a different address,
prices far below market, no GST bill, pressure to buy Schedule H1 in bulk.

## Every delivery — the 20-second check

- [ ] Batch number and expiry on **both** invoice and pack
- [ ] Expiry at least **12 months** away (reject anything less unless you'll sell it in weeks)
- [ ] Quantity matches the purchase order
- [ ] Cold-chain items still cold, indicator intact
- [ ] Invoice shows **your** Drug Licence No. and **their** GSTIN
- [ ] Schedule H1 items entered in the H1 register, with prescriber details
- [ ] Amount matches the PO — no surprise "handling charges"

## Ordering routine that stops stock-outs

A pharmacy that runs out of a daily medicine loses that customer to the shop
next door, permanently. The fix is boring and it works:

1. **One fixed ordering day per week** (e.g. Thursday) for routine stock.
2. **Urgent list** for anything that ran out — order the same day.
3. Keep a **"last strip" marker** on the shelf: when a fast-mover drops to one
   strip, it goes on the list, not to memory.
4. **One dealer per category** where possible: generics, injectables, surgicals,
   baby care. Splitting every order across four dealers loses you the volume
   discount and doubles the paperwork.
5. **Never order Schedule H1 on a phone call alone.** Written PO, written
   invoice, register entry. Every time.

## Legal line

- Only a **registered pharmacist** may dispense or sign the Schedule H/H1
  register. Not a receptionist, not an assistant, not a bot.
- Keep invoices for the period your Drug Inspector requires (commonly 5 years).
- A bot can draft a purchase order. A human with a licence must approve and send it.
